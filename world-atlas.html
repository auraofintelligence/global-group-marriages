Global research design

One family can cross several legal worlds

This is a way to ask better questions about marriage, family, personal autonomy and a group’s shared enterprise wherever its members live or travel. It is a proposed adaptation of Luke Nathan Hayes’s Australian citizen research, not a completed global law database or a verdict on any particular relationship.

Sketch a group in the Household LabHow evidence is handled

Marriage and recognition

Ask which relationships the place recognises, what it prohibits, and which commitments people can make without treating private agreements as marriage.

Families and care

Track parentage, children’s interests, guardianship, succession, housing, healthcare and responsibilities across the whole group.

Self sovereignty

Record each adult’s consent, privacy, independent resources, mobility and ability to revise or leave an arrangement.

Group enterprise

Distinguish the family from any cooperative, company, trust or other venture: membership, work, ownership, liability, accounting and exit need their own rules.

Law of the land

Follow citizenship, residence, the site of an event or asset, local rules, cultural protocols and any customary authority. A national headline alone cannot answer a local question.

A reusable pathway

From a lived situation to a sourced question

Each fork starts with its own people and places. The original Australian work supplies methods, while the local branch needs its own sources and review.

  1. Map the group and its places

    List members, citizenships, residences, children, homes, assets, ventures and planned travel. Note whose law or protocol may apply to each event.

  2. Use a relevance ladder

    Gather, chomp, sort, plan and act on what matters to those people. This adapts Luke’s 2012–13 Australian personal filing method; it does not make the old documents current law elsewhere.

  3. Trace authority and time

    Record a publisher, exact provision or policy, jurisdiction, version or effective date, and the date it was checked. Keep binding law, official guidance, practice, protocol and proposed reform in separate lanes.

  4. Test the practical instruments

    Check bills, contracts, property and business documents clause by clause: who pays, who may decide, what renews, and what happens on exit or a move.

  5. Leave unknowns visible

    Write the unresolved question, source still needed and person who could review it. Export a brief for a local adviser or a separately chosen research tool; do not turn missing evidence into a universal answer.

A record that travels without pretending places are identical

A group might form in one country, share care in another and own a venture in a third. Keep one scenario, then attach a separate evidence record to each place and authority. A local fork can change the questions, language and institutions while preserving links back to the original work.

Situation

Who is involved, what they want to do, and which person or entity makes each choice?

Place and authority

Where does the event happen, and which state, local, Indigenous, customary or other authority is relevant?

Evidence

What primary source supports the statement, what version was checked, and what remains uncertain?

Action

What conversation, document, professional review or fresh source check comes next?
